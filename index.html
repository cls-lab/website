We work at the boundaries of AI and the humanities, building and adapting computational methods as instruments for both measurement and interpretation.

We study the language systems that transmit and shape modern culture, from internet platforms to literary archives to language models.

Research

What we study

We use natural language processing methods and models to illuminate questions humanists and social scientists have long cared about, and to bring humanistic nuance back to computational systems. We frequently collaborate with interdisciplinary teams across the humanities and social sciences.

01

Pretraining Pipelines

Probing the curation and processing pipelines that underlie large language models by examining what texts are included, how they are weighted and altered, and what cultural assumptions are embedded before training begins.

02

Social Storytelling

Studying how people narrate their lives in online communities by modeling latent story structures, social norms of storytelling, and how stories contribute to collective sense-making.

03

Poetry as Data

Using poetry as a probe for language models by measuring how their formal features (line breaks, spacing, shapes) are transformed in modern curation and modeling systems.

04

Health Narratives

Analyzing how patients and caregivers tell stories about illness, both in online communities and in increasingly AI-mediated electronic health records.

05

Human–AI Writing

Examining how people use language models for creative and personal goals, including the personal information they share and how co-authorship with AI reshapes the acts of editing and writing.

Recent Papers

Selected work

01

Characterizing Narrative Content in Web-scale LLM Pretraining Data

arXiv 2026

Examines narrative structure within Dolma, a massive LLM pretraining corpus, using an 11-dimension framework. Finds that narrative qualities are unevenly distributed across data sources in ways current curation methods can't address.

Teagan Johnson, Elliott Ash, Andrew Piper, Maria Antoniak
02

so much depends / upon / a whitespace: Why Whitespace Matters for Poets and LLMs

EMNLP 2025

Examines how whitespace functions as a meaningful artistic element in poetry, finding that LLMs largely fail to replicate the whitespace patterns seen in published poems, with implications for how pretraining data is linearized.

Sriharsh Bhyravajjula, Melanie Walsh, Anna Preus, Maria Antoniak
03

Research Borderlands: Analysing Writing Across Research Cultures

ACL 2025

Studies cultural norms in academic writing across disciplines. Through both computational experiments and interviews with interdisciplinary researchers, shows how LLMs tend to homogenize writing rather than adapt to distinct research cultures.

Shaily Bhatt, Tal August, Maria Antoniak

01Are you recruiting PhD students?+

Yes, I am recruiting 1–2 PhD students in Computer Science at CU Boulder to start in Fall 2027.

02What are you looking for in PhD students?+

I'm looking for students with experience in (1) natural language processing, (2) software engineering or data science, and (3) academic research. My work is interdisciplinary, and I appreciate when students have experience in areas outside of computer science. I also value time spent in industry and/or pursuing other interests outside of academia. Students with non-traditional backgrounds are very welcome to apply.

This year, I'm particularly interested in students with interests in healthcare and/or literary studies, but I welcome applications from other students as well.

03How can I apply to do a PhD with you?+

You need to apply to the CS PhD program at CU Boulder and list me as a potential advisor in the application form. I can also advise and co-advise students in Information Science.

There is no need to email me. I'm sorry, but due to the volume of messages, I will probably not be able to reply to any messages about PhD admissions. I appreciate your interest very much — but for context, I receive at least one request like this per day, and it is simply impossible to respond to, let alone meet with, everyone who reaches out to me.

If application fees are a burden, check out CU Boulder's various fee waiver programs. Unfortunately I can't provide feedback on your application ahead of time or provide any guarantees about admission.
